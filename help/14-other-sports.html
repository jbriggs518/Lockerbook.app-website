Scoring Other Sports

Soccer has its own scoring screen (see Scoring a game). Every other sport uses the stat sheet: one button per stat, grouped the way coaches think about the game.

Preview: baseball, softball, basketball, football, volleyball, lacrosse, ice hockey, field hockey, wrestling, tennis, and bowling are in preview. They work for real games, and we're adding sport-specific screens based on your feedback. Email contact@lockerbook.app. Track & field, cross country, and swimming & diving are coming soon.

Baseball and softball: the inning header

At the top of the pad you get Top or Bottom of the inning, the outs in that half, the score, and a line score with runs, hits (H) and errors (E), visitors on top. Tap Out as outs happen; after three the header switches to the bottom half of the same inning, and after six it tells you the inning is over. Undo out takes one back. It's a marker for you — Lockerbook never ends an inning on its own, and the count never goes to MaxPreps. Tap Next Inning when you're ready. Tied after regulation? Keep going: extra innings are allowed until someone wins. If the home team leads after the top of the last inning, the header says so and you can tap Final.

  • Grand Slam is instead of Home Run, not as well: tap one or the other.
  • Pitches and Outs Pitched add to what the pitcher already has, and the entry shows "So far this game" so you can check the running total.
  • Win, Loss, Save, Start, Complete Game and Shutout can be given to a pitcher once per game.

Baseball, softball and football have more buttons than fit on one screen, so the pad groups them (Batting, Running, Pitching, Fielding, Catching) and you switch groups with the picker above the buttons. It stays where you left it.

One tap, both players

Some plays belong to two (or three) players. Lockerbook asks for the second one as part of the same play, and Undo takes back the whole play:

  • Football: after Pass Complete and the yards, pick who caught it — the catch gets the same yards, so passing and receiving always match. After Receiving TD, pick who threw it for the passing touchdown.
  • Basketball: a made shot asks who assisted (or No assist).
  • Hockey: a goal asks for up to two assists. Field hockey: one assist.
  • Volleyball: Block Assist asks for the other blocker (and a third, if there was one). It's one point, and each blocker gets a block assist.

Tap Not sure or No assist to skip the second player.

Before the game

  1. Open your team → tap the game under Upcoming (or Start a Game).
  2. Pick the starting lineup (or batting order, wrestlers, or bowlers). In hockey, lacrosse, and field hockey, swipe right on your goalie and tap Set G.
  3. Check the format: quarters, periods, innings, sets, or games, and whether there's a clock. Then tap Ready.

Recording plays

  • Tap Start (for example Start Q1, Start Inning 1, or Start Set 1).
  • Tap a stat button, then the player. Players in your lineup are listed first; the rest of the roster is below. Tap Player not sure if you didn't see who it was.
  • Some stats ask for a number:
  • Football: yards for rushes, completions, receptions, and punts. Tap Loss for negative yards.
  • Bowling: pins for a game.
  • Boys' lacrosse: penalty time — tap 0:30, 1:00, 1:30… (the number is in seconds).
  • Hockey: penalty minutes — tap 2, 4, 5 or 10. Use Bench minor when no player is charged.
  • Baseball and softball: outs pitched (3 per inning) and pitches.
  • A number that can't be right (a 301 bowling game, 700 seconds of penalty) is refused, so a typo doesn't change the stats.
  • Buttons that score show their points (+1, +2, +3, +6). The score updates automatically.
  • Opponent buttons record the other team's points. In hockey, lacrosse, and field hockey, an opponent goal counts against your goalie in net, and Shot Saved by Our Goalie counts as a save.
  • Volleyball: kills, aces, and solo blocks score for you. Errors (attack, service, block, reception, ball-handling) give the point to the opponent and are charged to your player. The header shows the set, the points it's played to (25, or 15 in a deciding set, win by 2) and says when the set is won. End Set asks first if the set isn't won yet, and a tied set can't end. The scoreboard shows sets won.
  • Basketball: the header shows team fouls this quarter for both teams, BONUS from the fifth foul of a quarter (the seventh of a half), and any player with four or more fouls. Technical Foul can go to Bench / coach.
  • Lacrosse: boys' teams see faceoffs and timed penalties; girls' teams see draw controls and green, yellow and red cards. Girls' teams start with 2 × 25-minute halves and 12 players (change the team's format if your state plays quarters).
  • Hockey, lacrosse, and field hockey: saves come only from the opponent's Shot Saved by Our Goalie, so nothing is counted twice. If you pull the goalie, pick Empty net when changing goalies.
  • The clock stops at 0:00 in every sport except soccer, even if you forget to pause it, so goalie minutes stay right. Football overtime has no clock.
  • Undo removes the last thing you entered (the whole play, if it had a second player). Long-press any play to delete it.

Box score and exports

  • Box Score shows the score by period and a table for each stat group, with team totals and percentages (FG%, 3P%, FT%, hitting %, faceoff %, save % and goals against average). A Not sure row holds plays entered without a player, so the team line adds up. A dash means there's nothing to divide by yet.
  • Export gives you a printable box score sheet and a spreadsheet (CSV).
  • MaxPreps: high school baseball, softball, basketball, football, volleyball, lacrosse, ice hockey, and field hockey all make the MaxPreps stats file, using MaxPreps' own stat list for your sport. Record the stats during the game and the file is ready when the game is final. See Sending stats to MaxPreps.
